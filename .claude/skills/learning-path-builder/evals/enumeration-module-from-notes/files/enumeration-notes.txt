Course reference material: Enumeration (Penetration Testing)

Enumeration is the stage after host/port discovery where the tester actively
connects to the services found and extracts as much detail as possible:
software and versions, user and account names, shares, directories, and
misconfigurations. The quality of enumeration decides how good the later
exploitation is.

Topics this material covers:

1. What enumeration is, and how it differs from scanning. Scanning finds which
   ports are open; enumeration asks each service what it is and what it will
   tell you.

2. Service/version detection as the handoff from scanning. Knowing the exact
   version of a service lets you look up known weaknesses.

3. Web enumeration: reading response headers, robots.txt and sitemap, and
   directory/content discovery with a wordlist. Finding hidden paths and the
   technology stack behind a site.

4. SMB enumeration: listing shares and users, null sessions, and what each share
   can leak.

5. FTP enumeration: anonymous login and what files are exposed.

6. DNS enumeration: zone transfers and subdomain discovery.

7. Turning enumeration results into a target list: which versions, which users,
   which paths are worth attacking next.

Note: the material is tool-light on purpose — focus on the concept of each
enumeration target, then show a representative command. Keep practice within
authorized lab ranges only.
